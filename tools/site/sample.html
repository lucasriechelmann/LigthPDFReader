A short tour of LightPdf

Reading PDFs without the weight

Most PDF readers have grown into suites: editors, cloud drives, sign-up prompts and pop-ups for other products. LightPdf goes the other way. It opens a document, shows it quickly and clearly, and gets out of the way. This short document exists to show what that looks like.

The idea in one line: open instantly, scroll smoothly, find and copy text, print, and nothing else.

What it does

Read

Continuous scrolling, smooth zoom, fit width or fit page, and tabs for several documents. Each file reopens where you left it.

Find

Search the whole document as you type. Phrases match even when they wrap across lines. Select text with the mouse and copy it.

Print

The standard Windows print dialog. Pages go to the printer as vectors, so text stays sharp at any printer resolution.

Why it feels fast

LightPdf is compiled ahead of time into native code, so there is no warm-up when it starts. Pages are drawn by PDFium, the engine inside Chrome, on a background thread. Only the pages you can see are rendered, which keeps a 400-page manual as quick to scroll as a single letter.

Measured on a 383-page manualResult
Launch to first visible pageabout 0.3 s
Drawing one page6 to 16 ms
Searching every page for a phraseunder 2.5 s, 145 matches
Scrolling, window maximizedabout 65 frames per second

Measured with LightPdf's built-in benchmarks on a Windows 11 desktop; your numbers will vary with hardware.

Reading at night

Night reading turns white paper dark and dark text light, but it keeps colors recognizable: a blue link becomes a lighter blue instead of orange, and photos do not turn into negatives. The rest of the window follows the light or dark setting of Windows.

What is deliberately missing

There are no annotations, no form filling, no editing, no accounts and no cloud. Nothing is sent anywhere. If you need those features, a full PDF suite is the right tool; if you just want to read, this is.

Open source

LightPdf is MIT licensed. It is built with .NET and Avalonia, and renders with PDFium. The source and releases are on GitHub at lucasriechelmann/LigthPDFReader.
